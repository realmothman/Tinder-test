# How to Conduct Dating App Research: Practical Methodological Guide

## Introduction

This guide provides practical steps for conducting ethnographic, qualitative, and sociological research on Tinder and dating apps. It addresses **how to actually do the research**, including sampling strategies, data collection protocols, ethical considerations, and analysis approaches.

---

## METHODOLOGY 1: ETHNOGRAPHIC OBSERVATION & PARTICIPANT ENGAGEMENT

### Overview
Researcher creates an authentic profile on the app and observes interaction patterns, interface design, cultural norms, and their own embodied experience over 6-18 months.

### Step-by-Step Process

#### Step 1: Preparation and Ethics Review
1. **Institutional Review**: Obtain IRB/ethics approval BEFORE creating profiles
   - Explain that you will create authentic profiles
   - Discuss consent challenges: other users will not know they're being observed
   - Propose strategies for minimizing harm
   - May require modified informed consent or waiver

2. **Researcher Positionality Statement**
   - Document your demographics (gender, age, race, sexuality, class background, location)
   - Explain how these characteristics will shape what you observe and experience
   - Acknowledge limitations of your perspective

3. **Research Plan**
   - Duration: 6-18 months (longer for deeper understanding)
   - Apps to study: 1-3 apps (Tinder, Bumble, OkCupid are common choices)
   - Geographic location: Which city/region?
   - Research questions: What specifically do you want to understand?
   - Expected data volume: Screenshots, fieldnotes, conversations

#### Step 2: Profile Creation and Fieldnote Setup

1. **Create Authentic Profile(s)**
   - Use real photos and genuine bio
   - Do not misrepresent yourself (age, identity, intentions)
   - Decide on disclosure: At what point (if any) do you tell matches you're a researcher?
   - Create fieldnote log (spreadsheet or notebook) to track observations

2. **Fieldnote System**
   ```
   Date | Time | Observation | Category | Codes | Reflections
   ---- | ---- | ----------- | -------- | ----- | -----------
   10/1 | 10:00am | First swipe session; feel nervous | Interface behavior | [first-time anxiety] | Why am I anxious about rejection?
   10/1 | 2:30pm | 3 matches; only swiped on 47 profiles | Matching patterns | [matching-rate] | Does the 1 in 15 ratio reflect studies?
   ```

3. **Data Categories to Track**
   - **Interface interactions**: How do you use notifications? Do they create urgency?
   - **Swiping behavior**: Patterns (left/right ratios), decision-making speed, criteria
   - **Matching and messaging**: When matches happen, what prompts first messages? From whom?
   - **Emotional states**: How does the app make you feel? Excitement, rejection, validation?
   - **Temporal patterns**: When do you use it? Morning? Late night? Duration of sessions?
   - **Social context**: Do you discuss the app with friends? How does IRL context shape app use?

#### Step 3: Ongoing Data Collection

1. **Daily/Weekly Engagement**
   - Use app naturally (3-5 sessions per week is typical for observed users)
   - Don't force artificial behavior; let your usage emerge naturally
   - Screenshot notable interactions (with permission/anonymization plans)
   - Write fieldnotes immediately after sessions (while fresh)

2. **Collecting Artifacts**
   - Save screenshots of: interface changes, notification patterns, profile examples (anonymized)
   - Document messages (keep exact text, plan anonymization later)
   - Screenshot bios that exemplify patterns
   - Collect app updates, algorithm changes, feature rollouts

3. **Interviewing Matches**
   - If you meet someone in person who consents: Can you interview them about their experience?
   - Informal interviews (30-60 min) about: profile construction, swiping strategy, emotional experience
   - Explicit consent: "I'm a researcher studying dating apps; can I use our conversation?"
   - Anonymize in writing

#### Step 4: Emotional Labor and Researcher Wellbeing

1. **Acknowledge the Work**
   - Dating app research is emotionally demanding (rejection, validation seeking, loneliness)
   - Regular reflection on impact to your own mental health
   - Seek peer support or supervision (another researcher to debrief with)

2. **Ethical Boundaries**
   - Don't pursue relationships specifically for data
   - Be honest with people you match with about research
   - If you meet people IRL, is research relationship parasitic? Disclose early

3. **Processing Rejection**
   - Low match rates are data, not personal failure
   - Document emotional responses (defensiveness, self-doubt) as part of embodied experience
   - These responses reveal cultural norms about desirability

## METHODOLOGY 2: IN-DEPTH QUALITATIVE INTERVIEWS

### Overview
Recruit dating app users and conduct 30-90 minute interviews about their experiences, self-presentation, emotional impacts, and outcomes.

### Step-by-Step Process

#### Step 1: Sampling and Recruitment Strategy

1. **Define Sample Criteria**
   - Target population: "Active Tinder users (3+ months use)"
   - Inclusion criteria: Adults 18+; English-speaking (or whatever language); active in past 3 months
   - Diversity goals: Intentionally recruit across gender, race, sexuality, age, location
   - Sample size: 20-40 participants typical (more for comparative groups, e.g., 25 women + 25 men)

2. **Recruitment Methods**
   
   **Method A: Venue-Based**
   - Post flyers at LGBTQ+ spaces, universities, coffee shops
   - Text: "Interested in dating apps? Researchers seeking participants for confidential interviews"
   - Include: Contact info, compensation offer ($20-50 gift card typical), time commitment (1-2 hours)

   **Method B: Social Media**
   - Facebook groups for dating, relationships, specific cities
   - Reddit (r/Tinder, r/OnlineDating, r/dating_advice)
   - Twitter: Hashtags like #TinderStories, #OnlineDating
   - Instagram: Post about study; use hashtags

   **Method C: Snowball Sampling**
   - Ask initial participants: "Do you know anyone interested in sharing their experience?"
   - Particularly useful for recruiting marginalized groups (LGBTQ+, specific racial groups)
   - Builds trust; reduces gatekeeping

   **Method D: Dating App-Based**
   - Some researchers message matches with research pitch
   - Ethical concerns: Can't be sure consent is fully informed when approached via app
   - Must be transparent and allow easy refusal

3. **Recruitment Script**
   ```
   "Hi [Name], I'm conducting research on how people use and experience dating apps like Tinder. 
   I'm looking for people willing to chat with me for about an hour about your experience—
   what brought you to the app, how you navigate it, how it's made you feel, etc. 
   Everything is confidential and you'll be anonymized in any writing. 
   I can offer [compensation]. Are you interested?"
   ```

4. **Informed Consent**
   - Provide written consent form before interview
   - Cover: Purpose of research, how data will be used, confidentiality protections, right to withdraw
   - Audio recording consent (if recording, get explicit permission)
   - Explain: How long they'll be anonymized (indefinitely)

#### Step 2: Interview Structure and Questioning

1. **Interview Guide** (semi-structured)
   
   **Opening (5 min)**
   - "Tell me about how you started using Tinder/dating apps"
   - "How long have you been using it?"
   - "What made you decide to try it?"

   **Profile Construction (15 min)**
   - "Walk me through how you created your profile"
   - "Why did you choose those particular photos?"
   - "What did you want your bio to communicate?"
   - "Have you changed your profile over time? Why?"
   - "Do you feel your profile represents your 'real self'? Why/why not?"

   **Swiping and Matching (15 min)**
   - "How do you decide who to swipe right on?"
   - "Do you have types? What are your criteria?"
   - "How do you feel when you get a match?"
   - "What do you do after a match?"

   **Messaging and Interaction (15 min)**
   - "What kind of first message do you send?"
   - "How do you decide whether to meet someone in person?"
   - "Have you met people from the app? How did it go?"
   - "Any memorable conversations or interactions?"

   **Emotional Experience (10 min)**
   - "How has using Tinder made you feel about yourself?"
   - "Have you experienced rejection? How did you handle it?"
   - "How much time do you spend on the app?"
   - "Does it ever feel addictive or exhausting?"

   **Outcomes and Relationships (10 min)**
   - "Have you had any relationships that started on Tinder?"
   - "Any hookups or dates?"
   - "If yes: How did those work out?"
   - "Any failures or negative experiences?"

   **Closing (5 min)**
   - "Is there anything about your experience we didn't cover?"
   - "How do you want to be identified? (name, initials, or anonymous?)"
   - "Any follow-up questions I can answer for you?"

2. **Advanced Questioning Techniques**

   **For Self-Presentation Probing**
   - "Tell me more about that"
   - "Why do you think you made that choice?"
   - "How might someone else approach this differently?"
   - "Have you noticed changes in how you present yourself?"

   **For Sensitive Topics (rejection, failure)**
   - Ask with empathy: "That sounds difficult..."
   - Normalize: "Many people experience this..."
   - Invite elaboration: "What was that like for you?"
   - Don't push if discomfort visible; offer to skip

   **For Intersectional Probing**
   - "Have you noticed your race/gender/sexuality affecting your experience?"
   - "Do you think people treat you differently because of [characteristic]?"
   - "How do you navigate [identity characteristic] on the app?"

#### Step 3: Interview Logistics

1. **Setting**
   - Private, comfortable location (participant's choice ideally)
   - Coffee shop, quiet park, university office, or video call
   - Minimize distractions; ensure privacy

2. **Recording**
   - Audio record (with consent) for accurate transcription
   - Use phone voice recorder, Otter.ai, or similar
   - Test recording quality beforehand
   - Backup recording to cloud immediately

3. **Duration**
   - Typically 45-90 minutes
   - Signal 10 minutes before end: "I have one more set of questions..."
   - Allow going over if rich data emerging

4. **Note-Taking During Interview**
   - Take brief notes (key phrases, not full transcription)
   - Use shorthand; focus on listening
   - Note non-verbal cues (pauses, emotion, body language)
   - Write memos immediately after (within 30 min): What struck you? Any themes emerging?

## METHODOLOGY 3: VISUAL ANALYSIS OF PROFILE PHOTOS

### Overview
Systematically code and analyze profile photographs using explicit coding scheme.

### Step-by-Step Process

#### Step 1: Data Collection

1. **Sample of Photos**
   - Collect 100-500 profile photos (more is better for reliability)
   - From public Tinder profiles (web-based access)
   - Or: Screenshot profiles as you swipe
   - Stratify by: gender, age range, location (if comparing)

2. **Ethical Considerations**
   - Anonymize: Blur faces or use only features (don't use full-face images in publication)
   - Remove identifying information (names, bio text, location)
   - Document: Where photos come from, date collected
   - Consider: Consent impossible (users don't know photos being analyzed)
   - Ethics board may require special approval for visual data

#### Step 2: Develop Coding Scheme

Create detailed coding rubric with clear definitions and examples.

**Example Coding Scheme for Profile Photos:**

| Variable | Categories | Definition | Examples |
|----------|-----------|-----------|----------|
| **Gender Presentation** | Masculine / Feminine / Androgynous | Based on clothing, styling, pose | |
| **Pose Type** | Selfie / Full-body / Headshot / Action / Group | Type of photo composition | |
| **Angle** | Eye-level / High-angle / Low-angle / Extreme | Camera angle relative to face | High-angle selfies suggest vulnerability |
| **Setting** | Indoors / Outdoors / Nature / Urban / Home | Where photo is taken | |
| **Clothing** | Casual / Formal / Sexy / Sports / Swimwear | Type of dress | |
| **Smile** | Genuine smile / Neutral / Playful / Serious | Facial expression | Duchenne smile (eyes engaged) |
| **Distance** | Extreme close-up / Close / Medium / Full-body / Group | How close to camera | |
| **Physical Markers** | Tattoos / Piercings / Unusual hair / Distinctive features | Visible body modifications | |
| **Social Cues** | Alone / With others / With pets | Who is in photo | |
| **Attractiveness Rating** | 1-5 scale | Conventional attractiveness (note: this is subjective; multiple coders should rate independently) | |

#### Step 3: Coding Process

1. **Coder Training**
   - If multiple coders: Train together on 50 sample images
   - Practice coding; discuss disagreements
   - Achieve inter-rater reliability (Cohen's kappa > 0.70 is good)

2. **Individual Coding**
   - Each photo coded independently by two coders (for reliability)
   - Use spreadsheet (Excel, Google Sheets) or qualitative software (NVivo, Atlas.ti)
   - Code all photos systematically (don't skip)
   - Document time: How long does each photo take to code?

3. **Data Entry Example**
   ```
   Photo_ID | Gender | Pose_Type | Angle | Setting | Clothing | Smile | Attractiveness | Coder | Notes
   001 | Feminine | Selfie | High-angle | Indoors | Casual | Genuine | 4 | A1 | Professional photo, makeup
   002 | Masculine | Full-body | Eye-level | Outdoors | Casual | Neutral | 3 | A1 | Gym/beach body display
   003 | Androgynous | Headshot | Eye-level | Urban | Formal | Playful | 3 | B1 | Professional headshot feel
   ```

#### Step 4: Analysis

1. **Frequency Analysis**
   - What % of photos are selfies vs. posed?
   - What % show smiling vs. serious expression?
   - Gender differences: Do men/women pose differently? (e.g., high-angle photos)

2. **Thematic Analysis**
   - What visual themes emerge? (Fitness, travel, adventure, domesticity, professionalism)
   - How do different genders/races/age groups present differently?
   - What message does the photo composition send?

3. **Comparison and Interpretation**
   - Do women use more high-angle shots? Why? (femininity, youth, vulnerability)
   - Do men display fitness more? (traditional masculinity, physical prowess)
   - Are there racial differences in presentation? (What stereotypes are being invoked or resisted?)

## METHODOLOGY 4: DISCOURSE AND TEXTUAL ANALYSIS

### Overview
Analyze written text in bios, messages, and conversations for linguistic patterns, meaning, and cultural themes.

### Step-by-Step Process

#### Step 1: Data Collection

1. **Bio Collection**
   - Collect 100-300 bios
   - From stratified sample (gender, age, location)
   - Copy text verbatim (maintain spelling, emoji, punctuation)
   - Record metadata: Gender of writer, age, location (if visible)

2. **Message Collection** (if accessible)
   - First messages / openers (500+ examples)
   - Conversation exchanges (30-50 complete threads)
   - Consent required: Only use your own conversations, or recruit participants who consent to sharing
   - Anonymize: Remove identifying details (names, specific locations, dates)

#### Step 2: Develop Coding Scheme for Text

**Bio Analysis Framework:**

| Category | Codes | Definition |
|----------|-------|-----------|
| **Tone** | Humorous / Serious / Flirty / Self-deprecating / Confident | Overall emotional register |
| **Content Themes** | Work / Travel / Hobbies / Relationships / Humor / Interests / Physical traits / Personality traits | What is the bio about? |
| **Relational Intentions** | Looking for relationship / Looking for hookups / Looking for friends / Unclear / Multiple options | What does person want? |
| **Self-Presentation Strategy** | Authentic / Ideal self / Playful / Mysterious / Professional | How is self being framed? |
| **Linguistic Markers** | Formal / Casual / Slang / Emojis / Punctuation | Language register |
| **Length** | Very short (1-5 words) / Short (6-15) / Medium (16-50) / Long (50+) | Word count |

#### Step 3: Coding Process

1. **Thematic Coding**
   ```
   Bio_ID | Gender | Tone | Theme1 | Theme2 | Intentions | Strategy | Length | Notes
   001 | F | Humorous | Travel | Work | Relationship | Ideal self | 35 words | "Wanderlust enthusiast, lawyer by day..."
   002 | M | Serious | Physical | Interests | Hookup | Confident | 12 words | "Fit guy, into hiking and fitness"
   003 | NB | Playful | Personality | Humor | Unclear | Authentic | 28 words | "bisexual disaster, bad jokes, good vibes"
   ```

2. **Linguistic Analysis**
   - Count emojis (frequency, types)
   - Identify slang terms, internet language
   - Note grammatical patterns (questions, exclamations, assertions)
   - Look for gendered language patterns

#### Step 4: Analysis

1. **Frequency and Patterns**
   - What are most common themes? (Travel, fitness, humor, career)
   - Gender differences: Do men and women emphasize different things?
   - How explicitly do people state relationship intentions?

2. **Thematic Analysis**
   - What narratives about self emerge?
   - How is attractiveness communicated?
   - What cultural values are expressed?

3. **Critical Discourse Analysis**
   - Who is absent from bios? (Disability, lower SES, non-attractive)
   - What stereotypes are invoked or resisted?
   - How do racial categories appear (if at all)?
   - What about sexuality and gender presentation?

**Example Finding:**
- Women use significantly more hedging language ("probably," "I guess," "maybe I'm the only one")
- Men use more declarative statements ("I am," "I love," "I do")
- Interpretation: Reflects gendered norms where women are socialized to be less assertive

## METHODOLOGY 5: NETNOGRAPHIC APPROACH (Kozinets Framework)

### Overview
Structured digital ethnography following Kozinets' 6-step netnography process.

### Step-by-Step Process

#### Step 1: Research Planning

1. Define Research Questions
   - What specifically do you want to understand about dating app culture?
   - What theoretical framework will guide you?
   - Example: "How do queer women negotiate gender performance on Tinder?"

2. Define Research Community
   - Which app? Which user groups? Which geographic region?
   - Boundaries: All Tinder users or specific subset?

3. Plan Ethical Framework
   - Will you disclose your researcher role? To whom?
   - How will you handle consent?
   - How will you anonymize?

#### Step 2: Entrée (Gaining Access)

1. Create Authentic Profile
   - Use real information
   - Participate genuinely in the community
   - Spend time observing before collecting data

2. Identify Community Norms
   - How do people interact?
   - What counts as "appropriate" behavior?
   - What are the cultural rules?

#### Step 3: Data Collection

1. Observation and Artifact Collection
   - Screenshots (interface, bios, messages)
   - Fieldnotes (daily observations)
   - Participate in related communities (Reddit, Facebook groups discussing dating apps)
   - Interviews with community members

2. Digital Artifacts
   - Bios and profile elements
   - Message exchanges (with consent/anonymization)
   - Memes and jokes about dating apps
   - Community discussions

3. Interview Data
   - 10-30 interviews with active users
   - Focus on: experience, meaning-making, community norms
   - Semi-structured (similar to interview methodology above)

#### Step 4: Interpretation

1. Thematic Analysis
   - Read through all fieldnotes, interviews, artifacts
   - Code for emerging themes
   - Group themes into patterns
   - Identify cultural meanings and values

2. Theory Building
   - How do your findings relate to your theoretical framework?
   - What new concepts emerge?
   - What do findings reveal about intimate digital culture?

#### Step 5: Ethical Standards

1. Informed Consent
   - Obtain explicit consent from interviewed participants
   - For observation/artifact use: May require ethics waiver or modified consent

2. Anonymization
   - Remove identifying details from all quotes
   - Change names, locations, identifying characteristics
   - Don't use actual screenshots in publication (paraphrase)

3. Data Storage
   - Secure encrypted storage
   - Limited access (only research team)
   - Delete raw data after analysis period

#### Step 6: Research Representation

1. Writing
   - Write ethnographic narrative
   - Include thick description (detail, context, nuance)
   - Use participant quotes (anonymized)
   - Reflect on your own positionality

2. Presentation Formats
   - Peer-reviewed article (typical)
   - Book (if sufficient data)
   - Thesis/dissertation
   - Conference presentations

## METHODOLOGY 6: AUTOETHNOGRAPHY

### Overview
Position yourself explicitly as research subject, using your own experience on dating app as data.

### Step-by-Step Process

#### Step 1: Positionality and Reflexivity

1. **Write Your Positionality Statement**
   ```
   I am a 32-year-old, cisgender woman, white, university-educated, 
   based in San Francisco. I'm educated in sociology/anthropology. 
   I'm relatively privileged: middle-class background, no disabilities, 
   desirable by conventional beauty standards. This shapes what I will notice, 
   experience, and how I'll interpret the app. I will miss experiences of 
   less privileged users. I need to account for my perspective.
   ```

2. **Identify Your Research Lens**
   - What aspects of experience will you focus on?
   - How will your identity shape your inquiry?
   - What commitments to reflexivity will you make?

#### Step 2: Data Collection

1. **Your Own Profile Creation and Modification**
   - Document decision-making: Why these photos? Why this bio?
   - Take screenshots of your profile iterations
   - Record your thought process in real-time or immediately after

2. **Daily/Weekly Fieldnotes**
   - After each app session, write reflectively:
     - How did I feel?
     - What did I notice?
     - What patterns emerged?
     - How did my body feel? (embodied experience)
     - What surprised me?
   - Be vulnerable: Document doubts, insecurities, desires

3. **Save Interactions**
   - Screenshot conversations (blur identifying details)
   - Save messages that reveal patterns
   - Document: When you get matches, responses, rejections

4. **Reflective Journaling**
   - Separate from fieldnotes
   - Write freely about emotional experience
   - Explore why you feel certain ways
   - Question your own assumptions and biases

5. **Artifact Collection**
   - Collect profiles that intrigue or disturb you
   - Save messages that exemplify patterns
   - Keep track of how app design affects your behavior

#### Step 3: Analysis

1. **Thematic Coding of Your Data**
   - Read through all fieldnotes, journal entries
   - Identify recurring themes in your experience
   - Code sections with themes
   - Look for: What surprised you? What patterns did you notice?

2. **Critically Examine Your Experience**
   - How did your privilege shape your experience?
   - What assumptions did you make?
   - Where did you reproduce stereotypes?
   - What limitations exist in your perspective?

3. **Theoretical Connection**
   - How do your experiences exemplify or challenge theories?
   - What do your experiences reveal about dating app culture more broadly?
   - What generalizable insights can you extract?

#### Step 4: Writing the Autoethnography

1. **Structure**
   - Opening: Position yourself, explain why your experience matters
   - Narrative: Tell detailed stories from your experience
   - Analysis: Interpret those stories through theoretical lens
   - Reflection: What did you learn? What are limitations?
   - Conclusion: Broader implications

2. **Example Excerpt Structure**
   ```
   "I created my first profile on a Tuesday morning. I spent 40 minutes 
   choosing photos—rejecting some for showing my body too much, others 
   for not showing enough. I was caught between wanting to be 'authentic' 
   and wanting to be 'attractive,' concepts that felt in tension. 
   
   [This phenomenological description]
   
   This tension is not personal but social—I was performing Butler's 
   notion that identity is iteratively constituted through repeated acts. 
   My photo selection was not innocent choice but performance of femininity 
   constrained by app affordances and heteronormative desire.
   
   [This theoretical analysis]
   
   The difficulty I experienced suggests that all dating app profiles 
   are sites where authentic self-presentation becomes impossible—not due 
   to individual inauthenticity but because the medium itself demands 
   curated performance.
   
   [This broader insight]
   ```

3. **Key Principles**
   - Be honest and vulnerable
   - Don't universalize your experience
   - Acknowledge positionality
   - Use theory to interpret, not to lecture
   - Let reader see your thinking process

## MIXED METHODS APPROACH: COMBINING METHODOLOGIES

### Recommended Combinations

**Ethnography + Interviews**
- 12 months participant observation
- + 30 semi-structured interviews with other users
- + Visual analysis of profiles you swiped on
- → Rich understanding of individual experience within cultural context

**Interviews + Discourse Analysis**
- 35 interviews with dating app users
- + Analysis of all interview transcripts for themes
- + Separate analysis of bios they created
- + Comparison: How do self-presentations differ in interview vs. on app?

**Autoethnography + Interviews**
- Your own detailed autoethnographic account
- + 15-20 interviews with diverse users
- + Contrast their experiences with yours
- → How does privilege/identity shape experience?

**Network Analysis + Interviews**
- Collect matching/swipe data from 500+ users
- Map network patterns
- + Interview 30 users about: "Why do you swipe for people like X?"
- → Understand both patterns and reasoning

## DATA ANALYSIS ACROSS METHODOLOGIES

### General Process

1. **Familiarization**
   - Read through all data (interviews, fieldnotes, artifacts)
   - Don't code yet; just absorb patterns
   - Write memos of initial impressions

2. **Initial Coding**
   - Open coding: Identify preliminary themes
   - What keeps appearing? What's surprising?
   - Create initial codebook

3. **Focused Coding**
   - Organize codes into categories/themes
   - Look for relationships between codes
   - Identify patterns (Who experiences what? Why?)

4. **Thematic Analysis**
   - Consolidate related codes into broader themes
   - Create final codebook with definitions and examples
   - Apply systematically across all data

5. **Interpretation**
   - What do these themes mean?
   - How do they relate to theoretical frameworks?
   - What insights emerge?
   - What's surprising or contradictory?

6. **Writing**
   - Organize findings by theme
   - Use participant quotes as evidence
   - Interpret through theory
   - Acknowledge limitations

### Tools

**Qualitative Analysis Software:**
- NVivo (comprehensive, industry standard)
- Atlas.ti (similar)
- Dedoose (web-based, good for mixed methods)
- MAXQDA (German tool, very capable)
- Open source: QDA Miner

**Spreadsheet Analysis:**
- Excel/Google Sheets sufficient for smaller projects
- Create columns for: Theme1, Theme2, Codes, Quotes, Memo
- Can do frequency counts easily

**Writing Software:**
- Overleaf (for academic papers)
- Google Docs (collaborative)
- Scrivener (for longer works)

## ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS ACROSS ALL METHODOLOGIES

### Core Ethical Issues

1. **Informed Consent**
   - Challenge: Users don't know they're being observed on app
   - Solution: Ethics board approval for observational methodology
   - Alternative: Disclose to all matches that you're a researcher
   - Consider: Is observing without disclosure ever ethical?

2. **Privacy and Anonymization**
   - Keep identifying information private
   - Use pseudonyms in writing
   - Remove location details, specific dates
   - Don't use full face photos
   - Be cautious: Can someone recognize themselves?

3. **Intimate Data**
   - Dating app data is sensitive: reveals sexuality, desires, rejections
   - Treat with care and respect
   - Consider: How would participant feel if their data was leaked?
   - Secure storage: Encrypted, password-protected

4. **Harm Prevention**
   - Could your research expose vulnerabilities?
   - Could it be used to harm people? (e.g., facial recognition research → targeting)
   - Consider: Secondary users (people in photos who didn't consent)
   - Plan for: What if your data is breached?

5. **Researcher Impact**
   - Using dating app for research affects real people
   - You might match with someone, disappear when data collected
   - Is that ethical? (Leading them on?)
   - Solution: Be honest about research when relationships develop

6. **Power Dynamics**
   - Are you researching "down" (studying marginalized people)?
   - If so: What's your obligation to those communities?
   - Representation: Who tells the story about their experience?
   - Benefit: Does research benefit people studied, or just researcher?

### Ethics Board (IRB) Preparation

When submitting protocol:
- Explain methodology (participant observation, interviews, etc.)
- Acknowledge consent challenges with observational data
- Propose solutions (waiver, modified consent, disclosure)
- Describe data security and anonymization plans
- Address: How will you minimize harm?
- Compensation: What are you offering participants?

## TIMELINE AND FEASIBILITY

### By Research Duration

**6 Months** (faster approach)
- Ethnography: 3 months intensive + 3 months analysis
- Interviews: 30 interviews over 3 months + 3 months analysis
- Could do both combined

**12 Months** (standard dissertation chapter)
- 8-10 months data collection + 2-3 months analysis
- Ethnography + 20 interviews + visual analysis
- Allows deeper immersion

**18-24 Months** (full dissertation)
- 12-15 months fieldwork + 3-9 months analysis
- Multiple methodologies combined
- Multiple research sites/populations

### By Resource Requirements

| Methodology | Time | Cost | Difficulty | Data Volume |
|------------|------|------|-----------|------------|
| Ethnography | 6-18 months | Low | High (emotional) | Moderate |
| Interviews | 3-6 months | Low-moderate | Moderate | Moderate-high |
| Visual Analysis | 2-3 months | Low | Low | High |
| Discourse Analysis | 2-4 months | Low | Low | High |
| Network Analysis | 3-6 months | Moderate | High (technical) | Very high |

## FINAL RECOMMENDATIONS

### Starting Out
1. Begin with interviews + visual analysis (lower barrier to entry)
2. Combine with literature review to ground findings
3. Use single theoretical framework (Bourdieu OR Goffman, not all at once)

### For Depth
- Add ethnographic observation (6-12 months)
- This allows embodied understanding of cultural norms

### For Critical Work
- Use autoethnography to position yourself
- Combine with interviews with unlike others
- Employ critical theory (Butler, spectacle, surveillance)

### For Ambitious Projects
- Mixed methods: Interviews + ethnography + network analysis + visual analysis
- 18-24 months
- Requires significant resources and support
