## How to Use These Documents

### If You're NEW to this field:

**Week 1: Orientation**
1. Read the introduction section of ETHNOGRAPHIC_SOCIOLOGICAL_FRAMEWORKS.md
2. Scan Part 5 (Synthesis table) to see how theories connect
3. Read ACADEMIC_SOURCES_QUICK_REFERENCE.md "Recommended Reading Order" section

**Week 2: Deep Dive into Theory**
1. Choose 2-3 theoretical frameworks that interest you (e.g., Bourdieu + Butler)
2. Read those sections thoroughly in ETHNOGRAPHIC_SOCIOLOGICAL_FRAMEWORKS.md
3. Look up 3-4 empirical studies using that framework
4. Read those papers (use ACADEMIC_SOURCES_QUICK_REFERENCE.md to find them)

**Week 3: Understand What's Been Studied**
1. Skim Part 2 of ETHNOGRAPHIC_SOCIOLOGICAL_FRAMEWORKS.md (published studies)
2. Identify what interests you: Gender? Race? Methodology? Theory?
3. Focus reading on 5-10 studies in that area

**Week 4: Begin Planning Your Research**
1. Choose preliminary research question(s)
2. Decide which methodology/methodologies suit your question
3. Read relevant sections of METHODOLOGICAL_APPROACHES_HOW_TO.md

### If You're DESIGNING A STUDY:

**Step 1: Research Question & Theoretical Framework**
- Read relevant theory sections in ETHNOGRAPHIC_SOCIOLOGICAL_FRAMEWORKS.md
- Review how others have used that theory (Part 2 studies)
- Decide: Will I use one framework or combine multiple?

**Step 2: Choose Methodology**
- Consult METHODOLOGICAL_APPROACHES_HOW_TO.md
- Review timeline and resource requirements
- Consider: Do I want to combine methods (mixed methods)?

**Step 3: Literature Review**

**Step 4: Method Design**
- Read detailed methodology guide in METHODOLOGICAL_APPROACHES_HOW_TO.md

- Prepare ethics/IRB submission

## Questions to Ask Before Starting Your Research

1. **Theoretical:** Which framework(s) interest you most? Why is this theory necessary for your question?
2. **Empirical:** What specifically do you want to understand? (Not "how dating apps work" but "how X population experiences Y aspect")
3. **Methodological:** What methodology best answers your question? Why that one?
4. **Positionality:** How does your identity (gender, race, class, sexuality, age, location) shape what you'll see and miss?
5. **Ethics:** How will you minimize harm? What consent/privacy approach is ethical?
6. **Contribution:** What's the gap your research fills? Why does it matter?
7. **Timeline:** How much time do you have? Is your methodology feasible?
8. **Resources:** What access do you need? Can you get it?

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Too Much Theory at Once**
   - Choose 1-2 frameworks, not 5
   - Show how they work together, don't just list them
   - Use theory to interpret, not to lecture

2. **Misapplying Goffman**
   - Goffman is important but not the only framework
   - Dating apps require updating his theory (permanent records, algorithmic mediation)
   - Don't just describe self-presentation; analyze power and inequality

3. **Ignoring Intersectionality**
   - Don't study "women" without asking: which women? What race? What class?
   - Intersectionality is not an add-on; it's structural
   - Broeker and others do this well—use as model

4. **Romanticizing Ethnography**
   - Ethnography is emotionally difficult
   - "Being there" is not neutral observation
   - Your presence changes what you observe
   - Plan for emotional labor

5. **Overstating Generalizability**
   - Qualitative research produces insight, not representative samples
   - Be clear: "25 women in San Francisco, mostly college-educated, white and Latina"
   - Don't claim findings apply universally

6. **Ignoring Ethics**
   - Don't skip IRB/ethics approval
   - Consent is complicated but necessary
   - Anonymization must be thorough
   - Plan for data security

7. **Undervaluing Existing Research**
   - These frameworks exist—cite them
   - "My study is the first to..."—is probably false
   - Build on shoulders of giants
   - Use ACADEMIC_SOURCES_QUICK_REFERENCE.md to be thorough
